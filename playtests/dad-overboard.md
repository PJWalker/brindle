# Dad Overboard — playtest notes (round 1)

There were three simulated tables. Each had one Keeper and two or three players.
- **Keepers** worked only from `js/seed.js`.
- **Players** ran as isolated sessions with no tools, no files and no web access. They knew only what the Keeper told them.
- All dice rolls were logged by a script.
- After each session, the players and the Keepers were interviewed.

## What worked
- The NPC quotes and "ways to play them" were excellent fuel. Players remembered three moments best: Pelletier's "I definitely won't mention the watch", Hollis trading the log for a pie, and Sorensen peeling his orange.
- The cold-store scene was a highlight at every table. It combined Etienne's nerves, the rug, the cod with the rat, and the talking fish.
- The complications and the first two Threats (*Set Sail*, *Body to Boston*) kept up the pressure.

## What broke
1. **No timeline of the night.** Every Keeper invented times, and the times contradicted each other. One table had the watch at 9:30 but Albert alive at 12:15. c3 and c4's "90 minutes off" never said off from what.
2. **The prep contradicted itself.**
   - Hollis's log: rel19 says it shows Sorensen, s6 says Etienne.
   - c4 says the watch is "on the body" but lists the Sheriff's office as a location.
   - The hook says "several nights", the backstory says "three".
   - s1 has Albert shot, but Finch's notes describe a blow.
3. **"Decide in play" clues** (c9, c11, c5, c16) put the steering on the Keeper mid-scene. Keepers felt their choices tilted the verdict.
4. **Dead ends.** c15 (the cod), c16 (the numbers) and c20 (Duchess) pointed nowhere, and players chased them anyway. Tommy, the church and the Gazette had nothing to find.
5. **Every table converged on Allison + Andrew.** Their clues are physical and easy to find early.
6. **Theorize was opaque and easy.** Players didn't know what "counted" as a clue or what the Complexity was. With 8–10 clues against Complexity 6, success was nearly automatic.
7. **The Void stayed atmosphere.** Players never learned what it was or how it connected to anything. Some first heard the word in the wrap-up.
8. **Missing basics:**
   - how to get aboard the yacht
   - access to the cold store
   - what the Sheriff's page says
   - Sorensen's terms
   - threat triggers
   - support for 7–9 / 10–11 results for each culprit
   - an assist rule
   - when to ask the paint-the-scene questions

## What changed
- **New "The night of the death — fixed facts" timeline.** It names no culprit. It covers the dinner announcement, the Rolex stopped by hand at 9:30, Sorensen's 10:10 launch (he waited and never boarded), the family's 11pm story, Sara's midnight splash, the unidentified 1am oars, the 4:30am tender, and the 5:30am discovery, plus Finch's findings.
- **Every open clue has a default** with alternatives, and a physical location.
  - c16 is the cold-store combination, and also a Void bearing.
  - c5 is in Duchess's collar.
  - c14 can implicate Sara.
  - c15 drives Etienne toward confession.
- The **contradictions are resolved**: Hollis logged Sorensen and heard the unlit rowboat, and the gun's bullet is in the panelling.
- **The Void connects.** The 1998 near-drowning off Brindle Point is tied to the firm's rescue. The chant is the same in the engine room and at the Point. Deckhand João's marks match one on Albert. The talking fish introduces the word.
- **New NPCs:** Gil Wexford (lighthouse), Prudence Lavoie (Inn), João Vaz (deckhand). Sorensen, Etienne's sons and the crew are now named and fleshed out.
- **Threats have "Tick when…" triggers.** Each solution now has a **10–11 wrinkle** and a **7–9 upper hand**, and all nine solutions fit the fixed timeline.
- **New Keeper guidance:**
  - what counts toward Theorize
  - announce the Complexity first
  - keep found clues visible
  - clue pacing
  - spread suspicion before a third clue points at one suspect
  - hold back the Allison/Andrew clues
  - an assist convention
  - when to ask the paint-the-scene questions
- Complexity stays at the published **6**. The difficulty is now controlled by what counts as a clue and by clue pacing. Raise it to 7–8 for a tougher table.
