# Keeper interview — G1 (Round 3), "Dad Overboard"

**1. What surprised me?**
- Dev stood on a stool and performed the moorings speech to the whole diner. I'd planned the Harbour Committee as a plain favour scene. I let it run as theatre and treated it as a down payment on Hollis's trust. Hollis's logbook and the unlit skiff then came out on a 12.
- Maureen reframed Lou's problem: "give her a story, not flowers." I expected a shy, minimal scene. I stayed quiet and let her enjoy it.
- Ruth kept the Sheriff's 4am secret off the table ("I'm keeping an index"). That's real table-knowledge discipline. I didn't expect it.
- Maureen asked on her own to go sit with the dog. I had been steering the lighthouse toward Gil and the pies. I let her go and gave her c20.
- Nobody boarded the yacht in three hours. I expected the yacht to be the centre of gravity, and I didn't push them there. Maureen's "we haven't been on it" was the first time anyone said it.

**2. Slice-of-life: what worked, what fell flat**
- Best: Lou and the carnations, Bunny against Vivian, Ida's tea, and Sara on the bench (five uninterrupted minutes in character). Each had a person in the middle of her own life, and the clue arrived sideways.
- Flat: Ruth's fish market. It was a clue chase with a miss on top, and she's the one who most wants to solve. The "ticking" scenes (T1, T2) felt like bookkeeping. They weren't scenes.
- Maureen: she opened up only after I handed her a personal hook (Marie, Lou's mother, her piano student). Once she'd laughed at the Chopin line she started proposing scenes herself: Sara tomorrow, the dog, pies for Gil. Without the hook she'd have waited to be asked. The seed's "ask each player for one personal stake" is what made her.

**3. Splitting became one Maven per scene**
- I framed every scene around one Maven, so the other two had nothing to do. Next time:
  - Frame scenes around a place and a pressure, not a person. Put two Mavens in the same errand with different angles (Dev charming, Ruth reading the room).
  - Give the audience jobs. They voice an NPC, make the weather, or decide what the room smells of.
  - Cut mid-scene on a cliffhanger and come back, as the seed's r-split says. I never did; I played each scene to the end.
  - Let a bystander walk into someone else's scene as an interruption. I never invited that.
- Honestly, three people each wanting spotlight pulls toward rotation. The rotation was mine as much as theirs.

**4. Lessons for future sessions**
- Hand over personal hooks before the first scene, not as a rescue.
- Keep the shared clue card list on the table and read it aloud at each cut.
- Decide the in-game calendar in advance (what happens Friday, when Finch is at the fish market).
- Pre-pick which Threat ticks on a miss versus which tick by the clock, and say which it was.
- Don't ask for a roll until the player has said what she does and which ability. It slowed nothing and kept the pitch honest.
- Put the Void thread's gate (c16) somewhere the players are certain to pass.

**5. One change to the seed; one to the Keeper instructions**
- Seed: write a one-page character-creation procedure (attribute spread, a short cozy-activity list, starting Condition rules, XP/advance list). Without it I improvised for 40 minutes of a 3-hour session, and everything downstream (modifiers, Complexity 8 odds) depended on it. If I could have two, I'd add "life right now" for Bev and the Krauses.
- Instructions: replace "actively split the Mavens up" with "give each scene a place, a pressure and two Mavens where you can, and keep a cut ready mid-scene." As written, "split up" plus "no rounds" produced rounds by another name, one spotlight at a time.
