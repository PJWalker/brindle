# Keeper interview — G3

One caveat first: I played the Keeper and all three players, so "surprise" here means moments where the character voices I was writing took me somewhere I hadn't planned. Dice were real. The table dynamics were my own simulation and probably friendlier than a live table.

## 1. What surprised me
- **Lu volunteering to judge the chowder.** I expected Lu to ask Finch questions or roll. She offered a favour instead. I had planned a Meddle for the autopsy facts, so I dropped the roll and let Finch talk, following "favours open doors". It spawned the Saturday chowder obligation.
- **Jo hiding the dog.** I expected the dog to be a quick clue. Jo kept Duchess secret from the group, and I had no rule for whether a clue found but unshared counts. I let it sit as colour and did not count c20.
- **Ellen reaching for "a spiral like the Sheriff's file".** There was no such thing in the file. I refused to confirm it, since a player's table knowledge about Albert's tattoo shouldn't become Dot's.
- **The six-minute chowder-recipe tangent.** I expected to steer back. I let it run and counted it as the cozy beat.
- **Frank giving Lu a late husband, Harold,** mid-scene. I accepted it and made the name joke.

## 2. Slice of life: what landed and what fell flat
- **Landed:** Hollis and Dot in the diner (four lines, ex-flame, nothing about the case); Bev handing Agnes twelve free pies, which was worse than being charged; Theo arriving with green hair; Emily eating pie in silence. All were driven by personal stakes the players gave me at session zero.
- **Fell flat:** the chowder-recipe derail, which was fun but unconnected to any decision. The loft reunion was mostly recap. Scene 4 (the kneeler and towel) was good but too short for how strange it was. The Void moment never had a test: no one rowed out, and nobody was offered a Crown.

## 3. Splitting and cutting
Jo made splitting easy; she wanted to go off alone. Ellen and Frank resisted, and I had to engineer separation with the pie errand. Cutting on a cliffhanger worked best (the kneeler turning to look at Agnes). Jo's secrets gave the reunion real friction, since the others learned about the prayer group but not the dog. But it also left the "single shared clue list" in r-split with no way to handle a hidden clue.
Next time I'd split the couple earlier and for a reason that pulls on them, such as Dot with Hollis, Lu with Finch. I'd also give each solo scene a countdown instead of just an errand. I'd pre-agree at session zero how a secret clue is treated at the table.

## 4. Practices for next time
- Time-box chargen. Three players took 45 minutes with a snack break, a quarter of the session.
- Decide the day and date up front and write it on the clock; t5 and t1 need it.
- Keep a one-line "open leads" card per Maven and read it at each cut.
- Use favour offers, such as pies, judging and ramps, as the main route into NPCs.
- Say the ability aloud before rolling. It worked every time.
- Let an NPC say "Void" in the fiction before using the word. Nobody did this session, and I missed the chance.

## 5. One change each
- **Seed:** a short character-creation page: ability values, a list of Conditions with an effect, and what an XP advance buys. Without it the first 45 minutes are improvised and the Conditions are decoration. The runner-up is a clue list with Finch's autopsy facts on it, since they're the best evidence and nobody can count them at Theorize.
- **Keeper instructions:** add a clock. Say how many clues a 3-hour table should realistically have, and either lower Complexity to match or say that a session without a Theorize is expected and what the Keeper does then (end on the threat tracks and the open leads). The "10-12 clues" and "Complexity 8" guidance contradict each other at this pace.
