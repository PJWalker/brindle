# Keeper interview — G4

## 1. What surprised me
- **Leon invented the torn diary page.** Irene saw the oilskin diary and said "a page is missing, isn't it?" Nothing in my description had hinted at it. I expected him to read the entry and move on. I asked him why he thought so, he said "the stub, the ragged edge", and I made it true. That gave the seed's Sara line ("a page is missing, c5") a player-authored anchor, and it was better than my reveal.
- **The spiral link solved itself.** I planned a slow mundane thread: tattoo, then Tiago, then the prayer group, then Sorensen. At the loft kitchen the three players put Albert's chest, Tiago's wrist and Sorensen's card together in about two minutes, in character, from three separate scenes. I had nothing left to hold back, so I moved the Void interest to the lighthouse.
- **Bea named Etienne from Harriet's scene.** Hearing "skiff" and "fish-market slip" in Dot's scene, she said Etienne. Nobody had said his name. It was a table-knowledge leap, not a Maven one. I let it stand as the table's theory but did not give Connie the knowledge in character.
- **Harriet's promise.** Dot promised Tommy she wouldn't tell the Sheriff, then told her friends anyway "because they're my friends". I expected either a clean promise or a clean betrayal. She found a third path, and I treated it as legitimate.
- **Leon reached into the water after a failed Composure roll.** I expected the miss to make him hesitate. He treated it as a reason to go further. The 4 became the story: the lost oar, the voice, then the Crown.

## 2. Slice of life: what worked and what fell flat
**Worked:**
- Bev, the wedding cake and "you leaned on it" (Harriet laughed for a full minute).
- Hollis's five-sentence speech and the cribbage peg that closed the feud.
- Pelletier asking Irene about Nadine while accidentally leaking the deck watch.
- The loft kitchen with pie and recap.
- Irene's tide-pools before Gil.

**Fell flat:**
- Connie's Inn scene until the miss. Chasing the guest list was a pure clue-grab with no life in it.
- The Sheriff's diet gag was a one-liner and never got a second beat.
- Margo and Hemingway got about two minutes. The cat was a prop.

**Balance:**
- Harriet got a Hollis thread across four scenes with a payoff. I gave her cozy first and let clues arrive sideways.
- Leon got a Void beat in nearly every scene he was in (the engine room, the diary, the lighthouse). I kept the Void to roughly one per scene so it stayed special.
- Bea got the sharpest edges: Bunny, Sorensen, the watch, the 11:52 call. Her best scene (Emily) was the one where she could be fast and clever. Her misses worked as rivals, not walls. She said "okay, that was good" after Emily, which was the signal.

## 3. Splitting, cutting, and the r-split / r-townlife conflict
Splitting went easily. The players did it themselves within five minutes, so I only had to cut. Everyone heard every scene (they were at the table), which meant the table knew more than the Mavens, and I had to ask for offscreen conversations to make knowledge transferable (Connie told Irene the numbers).

On the conflict: r-split says cut every exchange or two, r-townlife says never cut away from a personal beat. I resolved it by cutting on a cliffhanger or a completed personal beat, not on a count. Scenes ran 9-14 minutes with one cut each. Bea wanted shorter and Harriet wanted longer. I did not resolve that fully; I gave Bea a harder edge and Harriet the last word in the kitchen.

## 4. Lessons for next time
- Rule out the calendar before play. I invented Sunday/Wednesday/Friday on the spot and got lucky.
- Settle the first-hour budget. Cap session zero at 15 minutes by giving each player a fixed template (name, age, job, stake, three numbers).
- Create one shared clue card list on the table from minute one and read it at every cut. I did a verbal recap; a physical list would have made the Theorize count visible.
- Keep Void clues to two or three and let a Void clue tick t4 only once per scene. This session hit 4/4 in a single evening.
- Crowns: decide the mechanic beforehand. Next time, a Crown gives one concrete power, one cost, and one named step on the tide track. I would not let it go through on a missed roll without a price, and I would offer it as a choice after the miss, not before.
- Let miss-moves be the Void's favoured entry (the dying fish, the voice). They landed hardest.

## 5. One change to the seed, one to the Keeper instructions
- **Seed:** add a one-page "table kit" at the front: the calendar (what day each thing happens), a character-creation template, and a fixed Crown/Condition mechanic. That would have saved the 30-minute session zero and about five improvised rulings.
- **Keeper instructions:** replace "cut between groups every exchange or two" with "cut on a cliffhanger or a finished personal beat, and tell the table which clues each Maven actually knows." That settles the conflict and the table-knowledge problem together.
