# Keeper interview — g2

**1. Surprises.**
- Sam swam a quarter mile to the yacht (1:05). I expected a launch ride or a request to Hollis. I made her roll Vitality instead of waving it through, and the 7-9 got her seen by Tiago and Andrew. It was the best cliffhanger of the night.
- Sam pocketed a spoon "to see what happens" (1:27). I expected a roll. Andrew simply counted the spoons and said nothing, which I'd rather have used later.
- Sam promised Sara not to tell the Sheriff (2:18). I hadn't prepared a hard choice that bound a player to an NPC. It was the closest thing to a Condition I used.
- At 2:48 Sam rowed out alone at dusk and accepted the Crown with "Yeah, sure". I expected the table to debate it. I had no rule for what a Crown does, so I improvised: no roll, no cost shown.
- Gary, who waited for turns at first, produced the best personal scene with his own rival, Edmund Pike.
- Priya asked three times whether something "counted" as a clue. I wrote a card each time. I didn't expect that to become the table's rhythm.

**2. Slice of life.**
It worked best when a player-supplied stake collided with the case: Dot and Edmund's crossword quarrel at the Gazette, Winnie's pies becoming the key to Hollis's logbook and Gil's trust, and Pelletier asking Dot for dating advice while slipping about the watch. Those felt like the intended mode.
It fell flat in two places. Dev's phone call and the break recap were thin because nobody had a stake in them. The fish saying "Void" got a laugh, not a chill. I played it as a clue handout instead of building atmosphere first.

**3. Splitting.**
It went well. Three solo errands by 1:15 meant everyone was always in a scene they cared about, and cliffhanger cuts kept the others listening. Gary and Priya stayed engaged as audience. Table knowledge leaked twice (the orange peel; Andrew and Allison) and was resisted when challenged.
What I'd change: my cuts came on a rhythm, about every 8-10 minutes, rather than on a beat. Sara surfaced in Bunny's scene by my convenience, not by the Establishing answer: Sam said Emily and I ignored it. I'd also run a short regroup scene before the Point so the players could trade information in character, as r-split suggests. I skipped it for time.

**4. Practices.**
- Keep a one-line card per clue as it lands, on the table.
- Say the ability before the roll; talking is free.
- Put each player's stake and Establishing answer on a sticky note and check it at every cut.
- Use 7-9s for a threat tick or a hard choice, and 10+ for leverage.
- Build Void moments slowly, not as a drop.
- Run the break recap as one line per lead.
- Decide in advance what a Crown costs and when a Maven needs to roll for it.
- Plan the clock: creation plus pitch took 40 minutes of the 3 hours.

**5. Changes.**
- Prep (seed): a "First 40 minutes" page. It should cover quick character creation (the attribute array, cozy activity, starting XP), plus a numbered-clue treatment of the Void thread (1998 clipping, Gil's logbook, c16 placed somewhere findable) so it wasn't improvised. Fix the duplicate Philippa/Prudence and the Prentiss/Pratt contradiction too.
- Keeper instructions: pull "ask each player for a stake and use the Establishing answer" out of the long Town Life text into the first-step pitch and flow, with a concrete cue: "at every cut, check whose stake is next." It matters more than any clue rule, and I nearly missed it.
