Happy to give honest feedback. I'm stepping out of character now, so this is Sam speaking, not Dot.

1. **What worked best:** The fish market and the cold store were the highlight for me. Finch's line about the watch ("ninety minutes slow, somebody set it") and the weeping pollock gave the scene a creepy edge that felt different from the family drama. Emily on the bench was the emotional peak. Dot's guilt about not calling her, and then the chance to actually talk to her, was the part I cared most about. The cozy cat-feeding scene also worked well as a pause to reset.

2. **Confusion:** Mostly around the time of death. I didn't know whether the 11pm sighting was reliable, or what the Sorensen launch timing (out 9:50, back 12:40) did to the window. I also wasn't sure when a rolled "complication" would actually bite. At the Inn I didn't know whether pushing Sorensen would cost me anything. Investigation options were clear enough, though: the GM kept listing them.

3. **Assumptions I made:** I assumed Dot had a relationship with the town's kids as a retired teacher, which made the Emily story easy to build. I assumed Wyman was honest but out of his depth. I also guessed what Andrew's "Bordeaux" lie meant before anyone confirmed it.

4. **What felt off:** The Void clues (shifting tattoos, prayer group, weeping fish, purple light) never connected to the murder. As a player I couldn't tell whether they were red herrings or a second plot. Sorensen seemed sharp and menacing, but his employers were never resolved. The "Theorize" count also felt inconsistent: my own theory covered 16 clues by my count, and the GM counted 12.

5. **Theory and roll:** The solution felt earned, since Allison and Andrew were already in the clues. But I think the roll was almost a formality. A 12 against a complexity of 6 made the answer feel pre-decided. The epilogue confession was satisfying, though a little tidy.

6. **Leads I wanted:** The Caymans account and Emily's "reporter" contact, the "T." vet's letters, Sorensen's employers, and the Point's praying group. I never got to pursue any of them.

7. **Pacing:** Good overall. The split-scene morning was a bit rushed, and the Sheriff's office scene piled on too many new facts at once.

8. **One change:** I'd tie the Void clues into the murder somehow, or make clear they're a separate thread, so the ending doesn't leave them dangling.
