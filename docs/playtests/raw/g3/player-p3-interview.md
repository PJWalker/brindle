Stepping out of character. Here's my candid playtest feedback, as Bea's player.

**1. Best parts.** The fish market and the cold store were the highlight. Finch's "drowned men don't usually bleed like that first," and the Rolex reading 9:30 when the real time was 11:00, gave the mystery a concrete, physical feel. Sorensen's orange-peeling scene at the Inn was also great: a menacing, polite NPC who gives information without ever raising his voice. The cozy-scene hooks (the cats, the pollock) were good texture and gave Dot's player something to do.

**2. Confusion.** The biggest problem was the split scenes. Bea was at the Inn, the others at the docks, and I didn't always know whether I could still act on what the others had found. Also, the "Keep your own count of the clock" note on Gage's timing left me unsure which times were canonical. I never knew whether the Sorensen launch time (12:40) or Gage's 11:45 sighting was the key.

**3. What I assumed.** I assumed Andrew was the family's butler with a history, and that Allison had some relationship with him. Nobody told me that. I also assumed Prentiss & Pike was a real firm the Krauses used. The "Corporal Gage" letters I took as an affair with Emily, but the GM only gave me that in a late scene.

**4. What felt off.** The Theorize roll: I could've named a theory with almost any clue cluster, and the 12-clue count depended on which ones I chose to count. The final "Void" hints (the prayer group, the purple light, Allison's "ask the ones who pray") were never connected to anything we could act on, so they felt like decoration. The Sheriff's donation and the "T." letter also seemed to resolve offscreen.

**5. The solution.** It felt a bit too easy once Allison and Andrew were the obvious pairing: the butler and the wife with the will. The clues did point somewhere, but several (the stock certificates, the paternity sheet) served as red herrings with no real payoff.

**6. Leads I wanted.** The fish-market rat in the cod, Etienne's "Gage and Ruiz" timing, and the Ohio tourists filming the spot. I never got to follow them.

**7. Pacing.** Good in the middle, slightly rushed at the end, and the lunch-to-Sheriff sequence felt like it covered three hours in three messages.

**8. One change.** Give the Void a concrete thread to pull, such as the prayer group leading to Brindle Point, so the ending feels connected.
