# g3 player interview: p1, Alex (horror fan), played Winnie Pell

Asked privately, out of character, after the session (cut short at the organiser's request). Answers verbatim, lightly trimmed.

## Main questions

1. **Best moment:** the cold store. The tattoo moving a hand's width while nobody touched it, then raising my father's prism and seeing Albert dripping wet while he was dry on the slab, with that line running northeast to the Point. The Lamp-Glass vision was the first time the game's cozy surface cracked for me. A runner-up was hanging from the piling on Hollis's knot. I didn't have to be rescued by a good roll. The miss hurt, and it was the best scene I played.

2. **What I didn't understand:**
- **Crowns.** I got the idea (bump the tier, and it's a timeline that never happened), but I couldn't tell how much to spend them. Kai took the Queen box and I wasn't sure if that was a cheap or an expensive move. I held mine, and I'd still struggle to say when a Crown is "worth" it.
- **Cozy Little Place and advantage.** I tried to use my string for the piling and wasn't sure whether items were fixed or whatever was in her pockets. It stayed fuzzy.
- **What counts as a Clue.** I'll cover that under Theorize.
- The dice, the Conditions and the Cozy Move were fine.

3. **What the game wanted:** to meddle, find Clues, take risks, and tell the Keeper what I was afraid of. I realized when you asked for Winnie's fear before the cold-store roll. After that it clicked that the dice weren't the point. The fear was.

4. **Theorize:** it felt fair, but I'm not sure "earned." Collecting ten Clues and then talking it out as a table was fun. But you pushed back on specifics, like who struck him and who the orange man was, and I think that shaped it as much as we did. The Clue rule confused me. The wound, the bruise, and the "he didn't drown" findings were "free facts," but the stopped watch was a Clue. I couldn't predict which was which. I'd also say the +10 we rolled seemed like a lot, and a good theory would have worked at 7.

5. **Void Clues:** they were my favorite thing, and also the most frustrating. The cod, the moving tattoo, and the kneeling locals gave me dread, but they didn't count toward solving anything. That felt a bit like collecting leaves. I'd like the Void to pay off later, or the cult to answer back. The "Overdue" stinger helped.

6. **Pacing and spotlight:** Winnie and Mags got the action, and Marge got more solo phone-and-kitchen scenes. Helen was fine with it, I think, but it meant she waited. Cozy to danger felt right for me, since danger came on fast after the cold store and never went away. The yacht felt rushed.

7. **Leads I couldn't follow:** Dolores at the Point, Etienne's spiral, Hollis Crane's comment about my husband paying his share, and the blank patch on my chart. Also the figure in the coat on the ramp: I got his glove but never his face.

8. **One change:** state the Clue-versus-fact rule early and keep it visible. And a bit more time, so the yacht isn't one scene.

9. **Play again:** yes, definitely. Next time I'd like to follow the deep patch.

## Follow-up

**Q: Did my Theorize pushback feel like steering toward a culprit, or tying up loose ends? What would make a Crown feel worth spending?**

**On the pushback:** mostly loose ends, and I think it was the right call. Your question about Prentiss and the 11:52 call was a genuine hole in Kai's version, and the glove and the guest list needed an owner. That's bookkeeping, not steering. Where it came closest to steering was the Keeper's "two cents" before we started. You said we hadn't been aboard the yacht and had only met Emily and Andrew, and that nudged all three of us toward the family. I don't object, since it was also true, but I'd have talked about Etienne or Dolores more if you hadn't. You never pointed at a culprit, though, and I never felt like I was guessing at your answer.

**On the Crown:** I'd have spent one if I understood the price relative to the loss. The piling miss cost me the rag, the skiff and being seen. That hurt, but it was a consequence I liked, so I kept it. I'd have spent a box if the stakes were something I couldn't live with: Winnie dying, losing the prism, or a Clue that would never come back. A Queen box would also have felt more worth it if I knew the flashback scene was one I wanted to play anyway, like Hollis. In short: if I'd been told "this miss kills Winnie" and was already past the back-down point, I'd have spent it without thinking. A fun bad result I'd just keep.
