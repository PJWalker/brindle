# g3 player interview: p2, Helen (cautious), played Marge Tidewell

Asked privately, out of character, after the session (cut short at the organiser's request). Answers verbatim, lightly trimmed.

## Main questions

1. **Best moment:** The midnight phone call from Marge to the dispatcher. I made it almost as a throwaway, to feel safe, and you made it true and it saved Mags from the fall. Second best was breakfast, with the two widows saying Hollis's and Walter's names out loud.

2. **What I didn't understand:**
- **Crowns:** I understood them best after you explained them twice. I still wasn't sure whether I could use one *after* hearing the Keeper's narration, or only after the roll. I wasn't sure what a Queen scene would cost me, so I never used mine.
- **Conditions and advantage:** I got the idea, but I wasn't clear on how a Condition affects a roll. "Disadvantage where nerves matter" depended on how you judged it.
- **Clues:** I didn't realize that "free facts" like the wound and the bruise don't count. I thought they did until you said so.
- **Theorize:** I understood the arithmetic. I didn't understand that the roll has no ability bonus, or that we'd pick who rolls and then you'd just announce the number.
- **Cozy Little Place:** I wasn't sure when an item could be spent.

3. **What I thought the game wanted:** At first, careful pottering and conversation. I realized around the cold-store scene that it also wants you to take small risks and let the dice make things worse. When you said "you could die" before a roll and gave me a choice, I realized it wanted me to *choose*, not just play safe. That was what made me trust it.

4. **Theorize:** It felt earned, mostly because we built it together from the Clues. It felt a little odd that you told us the result so quickly, and that we'd voted for Kai to roll but I ended up rolling. And your "5 + 5 + 10 − 6" line lost me for a second. I didn't feel it was *too* easy, since we'd earned the Clues through risks, but the 12+ felt like a gift.

5. **Void Clues:** The tattoo moving was the creepiest thing in the game, and I loved the prayer group at the Point. They also made me a bit anxious, because they felt like the part of the game that was going to bite later. I was glad they didn't count toward the theory, since that kept the murder solvable. The cod saying "Void" is the one I can't stop thinking about.

6. **Pacing and spotlight:** Mostly good. Marge got her share, but I was alone at home during the dangerous night scenes, so I watched a lot of Kai and Alex's rolls. That suited me, but it might not suit everyone. The cozy-to-danger balance was right, with a little more menace than I expected. The yacht scene was short, which I'd call a pity.

7. **Leads I couldn't follow:** Emily at five o'clock (Andrew took the note), Dolores and the co-op office, Etienne, Hollis Crane's "paid their share," Walter's connection to A.K., and the keeper's cottage with the dark window. I'd also loved to compare the numbers to the safe sooner.

8. **One change:** A short written cheat-sheet at the start with the three move types and what counts as a Clue, so I wasn't piecing it together from your explanations. And I'd like a clearer signal of when a Crown can be used.

9. **Play again?** Yes, definitely. I'd like to keep Marge. I'd like to see what Dolores and the Void do next, and whether Marge can keep her husband's memory and her own safety in the same kitchen.

## Follow-up

**Q: Was watching Alex and Kai in danger stressful in a good or bad way? Did you ever want Marge out at night, and what would have made it safe enough?**

Mostly stressful in a good way. It was like watching a thriller with people I like. I was leaning forward, and I kept offering advice because I cared about Winnie and Mags. A couple of times it tipped toward bad. When you narrated Mags going over the wall, and the Ray Gilchrist line, my stomach dropped, and I started thinking about how to save her instead of enjoying the scene. That's the part I'd flag. It wasn't that anything was wrong. I'm just someone who feels it when a character I'm invested in is in trouble, and I don't have much to do about it from the sidelines. What helped was that you'd told us at the start that Crowns exist and that we'd get a choice before anything deadly, so I never felt blindsided.

Did I want Marge out at night? Once, a little: the cold store and the float. I felt left out, and I knew I'd miss the cool discoveries. But "left out" wasn't enough to get me to go. The Void tattoo, the locked freezer, and the ebb with a cliff behind it all had a flavor of "you might not come back," and I'm not willing to gamble Marge on that.

What would have made it feel safe enough: a few things.
- **A clear exit before I commit.** The deal you offered, hearing the stakes and being able to back down with nothing bad happening, is the big one. I'd want to be sure that backing down still keeps the scene interesting.
- **A companion.** I'd go out at night with Winnie or Mags, not alone, especially if one of them had better Vitality than my -1.
- **Daylight-adjacent danger.** Dusk, a lit wharf, or a crowded place, with the Sheriff nearby. Anything where the worst result is a Condition and not death.
- **A concrete reason.** A lead that really mattered to Marge, like Walter's payment, would have pushed me harder than curiosity.

If you offered me a night scene with those four things, I think I'd have taken it.
