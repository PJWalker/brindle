### App guidance that helped
- mv-night gm ("be honest... if she could be killed, say so now... go big") and mv-day gm (passive trigger on an unsettling Void Clue, "usually with Composure"). I used both passive triggers (Mags in the cold store, Marge in daylight). They were the best-received mechanic of the night: all three players named the "say your fear, hear how it's worse, choose" loop as the moment they understood the game.
- r-danger: CLUE JUST OUT OF REACH (the skiff bow on the slimy float), SABOTAGE after 4-5 Clues (the runaway ice cart), OBSCURED AGGRESSORS (coat, flat cap, black boot, gloved hand). The checklists were concrete enough to use live.
- r-gmmoves: "Show a Maven being killed: only on a miss, only if telegraphed" gave me the confidence to narrate Mags's fall in full. Kai: "That one stung, and I loved it."
- mv-crowns gm ("narrate it fully... their agency came earlier") was right. The Crown landed as intended.
- mv-cozy gm ("player names the Clue, add it to the list"); r-townlife USE THE ESTABLISHING ANSWER and MAKE IT ABOUT THEM (Emily seeking Marge out; Dolores at the Point; the chowder-judge happening as a 7-9 complication).

### Rules friction for new players
- Clue vs free fact: all three players miscounted (wound, bruise and blood type felt like Clues). This needs one explicit sentence at the start.
- Crowns: when (after narration), what each track costs, the Void boxes' effect, and that all must Crown for Theorize.
- Which ability: Kai asked every time. A one-line ability reminder on the move card would help.
- A Cozy Little Place: players forgot items they were holding (Helen with the green ledger) and asked whether improvised pocket contents count (Alex's string). The book answer (only listed items give advantage; others are fictional positioning) should be in the player-facing pitch.
- B.A. Baracus when the B.A. Maven isn't present: undefined in both book and app.
- Kai wanted a pre-roll trade (take a Condition for a better roll). The book doesn't offer one, and the app should say so plainly so Keepers don't invent it under pressure.

### Verdict on the style (danger-forward, by the book)
It works, and the book's Day/Night negotiation is the engine that makes it work. Being honest about death before the roll ("Mags can die here") produced the session's best moments and real Crown use. The horror player and the risk player were delighted. The cautious player (Helen) never went out at night, yet she was engaged throughout. She advised others, her suggestions became fictional positioning twice (tie on to the piling, the dispatcher call), and the game reached her at home through a soft miss (the 11:52 phone call). She said watching others in danger was "stressful in a good way", with one spike when Mags was shown dying. She'd have gone out at night with a companion, a guaranteed safe back-down, dusk or lit conditions, and a personal reason. Two cautions. First, danger plus split party left Helen with the quietest spotlight, so a danger-forward Keeper must deliberately bring danger-adjacent beats to the cautious player in daylight (the passive Day Move at the body worked well for that). Second, the dice ran hot (six 12+ results in 20 rolls), so the Crowns were used only once; the style alone doesn't guarantee Crown use.

2. Expand r-pitch's Crown line: "After a roll AND after I narrate, mark a box to raise it one tier. Queen (5, any order): you owe a scene of her life. Void (7, in order): the cult's mark; the 7th retires her. For Theorize, everyone must Crown." Add the same to mv-crowns' trigger.

5. Add a Lines & Veils flag to npc-duchess and c20 ("animal-harm Line? keep Duchess safe on screen; the blood is Albert's"). Add a line in r-danger for cautious players: "Offer night danger with a companion, a guaranteed back-down, and a personal reason; route daylight passive Day Moves to whoever stays home." Optionally add a note to mv-night: "No pre-roll trades in the book; the Crown and backing down are the levers."
