# g3 player interview: p3, Kai (Blades player), played Mags Thibodeaux

Asked privately, out of character, after the session (cut short at the organiser's request). Answers verbatim, lightly trimmed.

## Main questions

1. **Best moment.** Putting on the Crown after the cliff. You narrated Mags going over the wall, Dot's watch ticking, Ray Gilchrist finding her at twenty to six, and I could just say "no, that never happened." You rewrote it as "you were already not there," and the cost was the watch. The fish-cart ride with Colt was the most fun, but the Crown was the moment I understood the game.

2. **Confusions.**
- Crowns: I only half understood the Void side. I took the Queen and never really learned what the Void boxes do beyond "the cult's mark."
- Advantage: I assumed it meant roll three and keep two, but I never confirmed whether Chilled's disadvantage also took the lowest two. It worked out, and I still wasn't sure.
- Conditions: I wasn't sure whether I could choose to take one on a miss instead of the harder consequence. You said no, and the reason made sense, but I'd have liked to know up front.
- Theorize: I understood the maths late. I didn't realise that when we counted nine Clues, free facts like the wound and Finch's blood result didn't count, so we spent a minute arguing about it.
- Which ability goes with which move. I kept asking "is that Reason or Composure?"

3. **What the game wanted.** For us to get hurt, gather Clues, and talk to each other. I realised around the cold store, when you made me say what I was afraid of and then told me how it was worse. That was a Blades-style flashback. I understood it was about naming the fear, not the dice.

4. **Theorize.** It felt fair and earned, because we built it out of our own Clues and nobody gave us the answer. But it was a bit easy. With a 14 we never really risked being wrong, and I didn't fully trust that "your theory is the truth" meant what it said. The Clue count also had a vague rule, since Clues "had to be explained" and I was never sure whether Duchess was really explained.

5. **Void Clues.** I loved them, and I mostly didn't try to solve them. The moving tattoo was the creepiest thing in the game. The cod saying "Void" was funny and spooky at once. I wasn't sure what they were for, since they don't count toward the solution, and I would have liked some sign that they build to something.

6. **Pacing and spotlight.** Mostly good. The split in the middle worked, and I got a lot of spotlight, maybe too much. Helen had the quietest stretch, mainly the night at home with the phone, and I'd have liked more for Marge to do on the yacht. The cozy-to-danger balance was right, and I especially liked the morning scones after the cliff.

7. **Leads I couldn't follow.** The skiff's bow, which Winnie missed. Etienne, whom I wanted to push. The safe on the yacht, which we got to only by theory. I also wanted to search the study and see the other children properly. The time limit cut that.

8. **One change.** More teaching of the Crowns and Void up front, and a short reference card for moves and abilities. I'd also like more cost on the big successes. I rolled 13 and 14 three times, and a miss-driven story would have given me more to push against.

9. **Play again?** Yes, definitely. I want to see what the Void boxes feel like, and I want to find out what Hollis Crane knows.

## Follow-up

**Q: Concretely, what trade would you have wanted at the table tonight, and at which moment?**

Three concrete moments, plus one smaller one.

1. **The cottage roll at disadvantage (the one I missed with a 5).** I'd already been told I was Chilled, and I was about to roll Composure to hide with the torch coming up the path. I wanted to say "I'll take a second Condition, Shaken, before the dice, and in return I get to roll normally." I was choosing to be more hurt in exchange for a better chance. That's the Blades trade I kept reaching for. I only had the Crown, which comes after the roll and costs a scene, and it felt like too big a hammer for a roll I hadn't even made.

2. **The swim after the sailor (the 14).** I was happy with a clean win, but with that much on the line I wanted to offer a cost up front: "Mags leaves the windbreaker and the slip with Winnie, and she goes in with nothing." Or even "I'll mark a Void box if that gives me a real look at the sailor's face." I wanted to push the roll, not just hope for it.

3. **The cold store spiral (the 13).** Same idea at a smaller scale. I'd already spent Dot's watch for advantage, which was great, and I liked that. But I wanted a second lever, something like "I'll take Shaken in advance to also get a free question about the spiral."

4. **Colt.** I held it too long out of caution, and at the fish cart I wanted to trade something for it. A flat "automatic 12+" has no cost in it, so I'd like to be able to say "yes, and I'll pay with a Condition, to get a bigger result than the cart just missing us."

What I felt was missing is a visible dial between "roll and see" and "Crown after." I'd put the price on the front, before the dice, so a 10+ feels like something I bought.
