# Player interview: p2 (Priya, played Dee Whitcombe-Rao)

Game g1, Dad Overboard, "By the book" style. Interviewed separately, out of character, after the session (which was cut short at the organiser's request just before the lighthouse/Point scenes). Answers verbatim, lightly trimmed of formatting.

## Main interview (questions 1-9)

1. **Best moment:** Laying the vase shards out on a napkin and seeing the gilt foot match the wound. It was the first time I felt clever instead of lucky. Close second was "Hold out your hands, Mr. Krause," because I had all fourteen Clues behind me.

2. **Confusing:** Mostly fine on the dice. Advantage and disadvantage clicked because they're the same as 5e. What I didn't get: Crowns (I still don't really know what they are), and Conditions, which came up as a surprise when Thea got Unnerved. I understood Theorize in outline, but not what counts as "incorporated." I assumed it meant "mentioned," and you never corrected me. Also, I never rolled, and part of me missed that. I know it was a rules-of-the-exercise thing, but it's a big change from 5e.

3. **What it wanted:** I thought it wanted me to *find the answer*. I realised it didn't around the time you said there's no set solution, and really understood when you said the theory becomes true if the roll hits. I was uneasy about that for a while. "Getting it right" turned out to mean "making a coherent story out of what we found," and that was actually more satisfying than guessing.

4. **Theorize:** Fair, yes. Earned? Partly. With 14 Clues the lowest possible roll was 10, so there was no tension in the dice. The work was in the discussion, and that part was earned. I'd have liked to feel some risk, even if we'd had fewer Clues. I also didn't know what counted until you did the maths out loud.

5. **Void Clues:** The parrot saying "Overdue" gave me chills. The chanting did too, and I loved that Dee refused to admit it. But I didn't know what to *do* with them. As a clue-finder, a clue that doesn't help is frustrating, even though I understand why they're separate.

6. **Pacing:** I got plenty of spotlight, probably the most Meddling Moves. The cozy scenes were a nice break and the Cozy Move was a great mechanic. The ending felt a bit rushed. We skipped the lighthouse.

7. **Leads I couldn't follow:** The lighthouse and Gil, Sorensen at the Inn, the crossed-out name's handwriting, and the missing jigsaw piece. I wanted to search more rooms than the game let me.

8. **One change:** Explain Crowns and Conditions earlier, and let players roll their own dice.

9. **Play again:** Yes. Definitely, and I want my lighthouse piece back.

## Follow-up

**Keeper:** You assumed "incorporated" meant "mentioned": when would a correction have helped, and would it have changed your theory? Would you trade fewer Clues for a tense Theorize roll?

**When a correction would have helped:** Right after I posted my first rough theory, or just before the open discussion, when you read the Theorize rule aloud. That was the moment I was deciding how carefully to account for everything. By the time you told us the maths, we'd already agreed on a story and it felt too late to rework it.

**Would I have changed my theory?** Yes, a bit. I mostly *did* try to explain each Clue, but a couple were waved away. Tommy's letter and Duchess became "red herrings," and I never really explained Albert's shovel at the Point five years ago, or what the thing thrown over the rail was. If I'd known "explained, not named" was the standard, I'd have spent more time there, and I'd have been more upfront with Sam and Ruth that I was hand-waving.

**Fewer Clues or more risk?** Honestly, fewer Clues would have felt worse. As a searcher, each Clue is a small win and the whole session is built on that. What I'd actually want is the *choice*: let us Theorize at, say, 7 or 8 Clues for a tenser roll, or keep searching for a safer one. That way the risk is something we choose, not something the game takes away from us.
