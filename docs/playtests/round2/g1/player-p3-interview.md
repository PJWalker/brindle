# Player interview: p3 (Ruth, played Winnie Pruitt)

Game g1, Dad Overboard, "By the book" style. Interviewed separately, out of character, after the session (which was cut short at the organiser's request just before the lighthouse/Point scenes). Answers verbatim, lightly trimmed of formatting.

## Main interview (questions 1-9)

Thank you for asking. I'll be as honest as I can, though I'm a polite person by nature, so do push me if I'm being too nice.

**1. Best moment:** Sitting beside Emily with the cat in her lap and just knitting. Being allowed to be kind and have that count as investigating surprised me. A close second was inventing Arthur's clock remark during the yarn scene. I'd never have thought of it if I'd been asked to produce a clue cold.

**2. What I didn't understand:** The dice, mostly. You rolled for me, so I never saw them, and I still don't properly know why "7-9" is different from "10+" beyond "a bit worse." Advantage and disadvantage I followed. Crowns I really didn't get. They were mentioned once, as homework, and I couldn't tell you what one is for. Conditions came late, and Sam had to ask. A Cozy Little Place was fine, because it was just objects. I wasn't sure at first which ability to name for an action. You helped, but I felt I was guessing.

**3. What the game wanted:** At first I thought it wanted me to find the answer, like Christie. I realised around the first Cozy scene that it wanted us to make the answer and the characters together. That was a relief, because I'm hopeless at spotting the vicar.

**4. Theorize:** It felt friendly, almost too friendly. With 14 Clues, I knew we couldn't really fail, and I wondered whether it had been earned or just allowed. I liked making the story fit, but "incorporated" confused me. I didn't know if a Clue counted if I merely mentioned it, or if it had to actually be explained. A short example would have helped.

**5. Void Clues:** I loved the parrot. The dream and the chanting happened to the others, so I felt a bit outside them. They were creepy, though. I was never sure why they didn't count towards solving anything.

**6. Pacing and spotlight:** I got plenty, perhaps more than I expected, and you kept inviting me in. Cozy against danger was balanced for me. I wanted more danger, honestly. Nothing frightening happened to Winnie.

**7. Leads I couldn't follow:** The lighthouse and the dog at the Point, Etienne's skiff, and the locked drawer. I'd have liked an hour more.

**8. One change:** Show the dice, and explain the key terms on one card at the start.

**9. Play again:** Yes, certainly. I'd like to find out what's in that drawer.

## Follow-up

**Keeper:** Theorize felt "almost too friendly": would you rather the roll could really go wrong? What sort of danger would you have enjoyed for Winnie?

I think so, yes, though I'd want it to be a story rather than a punishment. In a Christie, the detective is sometimes wrong for a chapter and has to eat her words. That's half the fun. With 14 Clues and a guaranteed hit, I never felt any tension at the table. If I'd explained only six or seven, and the dice had gone against us, I'd have found it exciting to accuse the wrong person and watch it unravel. I wouldn't want to lose the whole evening, though. Being wrong and then fixing it, with something at stake, would be fine. Being wrong and having nowhere to go would feel like a slap, and since I'm new and nervous, I'd probably have gone quiet.

For danger, I'd have liked the cozy kind of peril: a locked door, a flickering light, the walk back along the dock with someone's footsteps behind you. For example, Winnie shut in the cold store at the fish market after Etienne clanged that steel door, or a cup of tea at the yacht that she suddenly isn't sure about, or the man with the orange simply *standing* outside her house. I'd want it to be tense for a minute and then get us out of it cleverly, with the cat or a peppermint or something from the card catalogue. Nothing graphic, and nothing at all happening to Mr. Biscuit. A little fear, as long as it's the sort Winnie can think her way out of.
