# Player interview: p1 (Sam, played Thea Vance)

Game g1, Dad Overboard, "By the book" style. Interviewed separately, out of character, after the session (which was cut short at the organiser's request just before the lighthouse/Point scenes). Answers verbatim, lightly trimmed of formatting.

## Main interview (questions 1-9)

**1. Best moment.** The Theorize discussion and confrontation. Mapping 14 Clues onto one story, and then Thea saying "your hair is going grey, Allison, and it still hasn't taught you to put the vase down", felt earned. Runner-up: Thea's dock-bench Cozy Move with Dee, where I got to invent the logbook Clue myself. It turned out to be the one that placed the skiff.

**2. What I didn't understand.** Conditions and Crowns, mostly. I learned what Unnerved did only after it landed on me, and I asked about disadvantage and clearing it, which you answered, but I'd have wanted that up front. Crowns first came up at the very end ("on a bad roll you can mark one to bump the result a tier"), after I'd already missed twice. I never had the option, so I can't judge them. I also still don't know what 5 XP buys. Advantage, Meddling, and Cozy Little Place were clear within one use each. Cozy Move needed one clarification.

**3. What the game wanted.** For me it clicked when you said there was no hidden answer and we'd build one from Clues. Before that I was treating it like Monster of the Week, where a mystery has a shape you uncover. Here the table writes it, and I liked that, though I had to switch gears.

**4. Theorize.** It felt earned, but not tense. At 14 Clues the minimum roll was 10, so it was effectively automatic. The tension was in the discussion, which was good, but I'd have liked a reason to stop investigating earlier, or a cost for dithering. The rule that a Clue only counts if you explain it is clear. The hard part was making Dee's and Winnie's different versions fit together, and your four holes did that.

**5. Void Clues.** Loved them. The parrot saying "Overdue" was the best one: tiny, domestic, and wrong. They worked because they didn't count toward the solution. I wasn't tempted to treat them as evidence and could just be unnerved. I wanted slightly more Thea-specific ones, since I took Dale Cooper for them.

**6. Pacing and spotlight.** I got enough, but group actions blurred it. When all three searched the alley or the house, one person rolled, and the Dee/Thea swap because of my Unnerved meant I rolled fewer times than I expected. I never used Sensitivity +3 for much beyond the rector. Cozy versus danger leaned cozy; nobody picked "real danger" and nothing really threatened us, which fit the tone.

**7. Leads I couldn't follow.** The lighthouse, Gil, and Duchess, cut for time, which is fair. I also wanted to follow the Sorensen-heading-to-the-Inn lead and see the Point at dusk.

**8. One change.** Explain Conditions and Crowns before the first roll, in thirty seconds. Everything else I could adapt to as we went.

**9. Play again?** Yes, definitely. I want to see Sorensen again, find out what's listening to Thea's attic, and try a Crown.

## Follow-up

**Keeper:** You said you'd have liked "a reason to stop investigating earlier, or a cost for dithering." Concretely, what would that have looked like? And would you still have Theorized at 14 if it had been riskier?

A clock tied to the fiction, not a mechanical penalty. We already had natural ones that never ticked: the body shipping back to Boston, the Ball on Saturday, the lawyers calling the Sheriff twice a day. If you'd said at the fish market "Albert goes on the afternoon truck to Boston, and once he's gone Dr. Finch can't do his proper autopsy," I'd have felt every stop at the yacht. After the first Clue each morning, the afternoon would get shorter, and I'd have had to choose between the Point and the loft.

I'd pick that over fewer Clues per roll or harder rolls. The Clues were the fun part, and making them scarcer would have punished the cozy scenes that gave us some of the best ones, like the dock bench and the jigsaw. I'd also be wary of you pushing us to Theorize at 6 or 7. That's more arbitrary than a clock, and it makes me feel steered. It would feel better if the fiction pushed and I chose.

Would I still Theorize at 14 if it were riskier? Probably yes, but I'd have thought harder about it. A hit at 10+ was effectively guaranteed with all 14 explained, so there was no reason to hold back except my own curiosity. If more Clues made the confrontation safer, say a higher chance that someone isn't hurt or gets away, then the clock would matter, because I'd be trading safety against time.

One caveat: I played one session. The game may intend the cost to be the real-time length of the session, not something in the fiction. You did nudge us at the end for time, and that worked fine. I'd just rather have seen it coming earlier.
