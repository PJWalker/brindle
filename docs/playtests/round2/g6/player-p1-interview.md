# Player p1 interview: Imogen (story-game veteran)

Interviewed separately with say.sh after Stars & Wishes, out of character. Answers verbatim (lightly trimmed).

## Main questions

1. **Best moment:** Turning over the "K" card and writing Walter's note on the back: "Don't write it down, Dot. It reads back." I invented it, and it hit me as if someone else had. A close second was the Night Move, when I said what Dot was afraid of and you made it worse using exactly that fear.

2. **Confusions:** Mostly mechanical. I never fully grasped how Crowns work in play, since I only watched Margaret use one. Advantage was clear, but I wasn't sure which Cozy items I'd already "spent." I also didn't understand Complexity until Theorize, when it turned out that 3 Clues were required and we had 14. Then I wondered why we'd had Complexity 6 at all.

3. **What the game wanted:** Authorship, handed over at moments I couldn't predict. I realised at the Sheriff's grudge question, when you asked me to invent Dot's history with him. After that I started grabbing the brush on my own, and it was welcomed.

4. **Theorize:** It felt earned in the discussion but not in the roll. We could add details to make Clues fit, so Clues were almost never a problem, and the roll was +14 against a threshold of 6. With that margin the outcome was nearly guaranteed. I didn't feel like a bad roll could have mattered. I did understand what counted, but I stretched things ("the rug sat in the anchor locker") to make them fit.

5. **Void Clues:** I loved them, especially the ones tied to my character. The spiral-card-slip-kneelers thread felt like a real current underneath the cozy surface. But they also felt separate from the murder, which was fine, though I wanted the cult to touch the case instead of watching it from beside.

6. **Pacing and spotlight:** Good. I had enough, probably a bit more than the others because I'm pushy. Raj was quieter, and I think he'd have liked a few more prompts. The cozy/danger balance was right; the night scene at the window was the best tonal swerve.

7. **Unfollowed leads:** The skiff at 12:15, the kneelers at Brindle Point, the journalist Emily is helping, and what Walter wrote.

8. **One change:** Make Theorize riskier, perhaps by requiring one Clue that contradicts the favoured theory.

9. **Play again?** Absolutely. I want to see where Dot goes with Walter's card.

## Follow-up

**Q:** You said the roll felt guaranteed. If inventing details makes almost any Clue fit, what would make Theorize feel risky without taking away the authorship you enjoyed?

I'd keep the authorship and put a price on it, so inventing becomes a gamble instead of free glue.

1. **Each retcon costs something.** Every detail we add to make a Clue fit, like the anchor locker or Sorensen being "on a lower deck," counts as a -1 to the roll. We'd still have the freedom, but every invention would be a choice, and a tidy theory built from real Clues would roll better than a stitched-together one. I'd feel that tension at the table.

2. **One locked Clue.** The Keeper names one Clue that has to be explained exactly as found, with no retcon. For us it would have been the Rolex or the rug's timing. That's the "one Clue refuses to fit" I mentioned, and it would force a real argument.

3. **Fewer Clues, same threshold.** We had 14 against a need of 3, which is what broke it. If we'd Theorized at 6 or 7, the roll would've mattered. A softer version is for the Keeper to say plainly, "You could Theorize now, but you'll be rolling thin," so the choice to wait feels like a gamble.

4. **Stakes on a miss.** I couldn't tell what a failure would cost us. If a wrong theory meant, say, accusing Andrew in front of Emily and having it be false, I'd have been much more careful.
