# Player p2 interview: Raj (freezes when asked to invent)

Interviewed separately with say.sh after Stars & Wishes, out of character. Answers verbatim (lightly trimmed).

## Main questions

1. **Best moment:** Dot handing Andrew the pressed sea-rose and saying "You can put the scrubbing brush down." Close second was Winnie's locked cabinet turning out to be "a girl's unwritten book." I picked that object off a list without knowing it mattered, and it landed hardest of anything.

2. **What I didn't understand:**
- **Crowns:** I still don't fully get the cost. I understood "bump the result, owe a scene," but not how often you'd want to.
- **Advantage:** I got "roll three, keep two" once you explained it, but I never worked out how many of my Cozy items I could burn, or whether I should save them.
- **Conditions:** I only really understood them when Winnie got GUILTY and then cleared it.
- **Theorize:** the formula was fine, but the "counts if it has a place in the story" bit took me a while.
- **My own dice:** I said what Harriet tried, and results came back. I'm not sure I ever felt the numbers.

3. **What the game wanted:** at first I thought it wanted a good guess. Around Theorize I realised it wanted us to build the answer together, not find a hidden one. The turning point was you saying "nobody, including me, knows." Even then I kept wanting to be right, which is my TV habit.

4. **Theorize:** it felt earned in story terms, because we argued, Imogen switched sides, and the "both" answer needed everyone. But the roll was anticlimactic: fourteen Clues against Complexity 6, and a snake-eyes 10. I never felt it could go wrong. I also only half-understood that I could add details to make a Clue fit until you said so in the middle of it. Earlier would have helped.

5. **Void Clues:** I loved them, and they gave me chills. The kneelers on the rocks and Walter's card were the best. But Harriet barely got to touch any of it. Dot got the shadows, and I mostly got a purse with a spiral card in it. I wanted something creepy that was mine.

6. **Pacing and spotlight:** I got plenty. Harriet collected a lot of Clues, and I worried about hogging them. Cozy versus danger was a good mix, and the diner and greenhouse scenes were my favourites. The danger felt more like atmosphere than threat. Nobody was really at risk, except Dot's hip.

7. **Leads I couldn't follow:** the skiff at 12:15 with no lights from the fish-market slip, which nobody ever explained. The Hendersons, who rent the dock and were at the middle of the rug story. Emily's journalist. And Hollis and Gil, which I started myself and would have loved to see resolved.

8. **One change:** a short, plain "here's what counts as a Clue" reminder before Theorize, plus defaulting to options for every invent-it question.

9. **Play again:** yes, definitely.

**On inventing all night:** I was nervous at first, then relaxed. The lettered options helped a lot, and I don't think they were a crutch. They got me started, and I often added my own bit afterwards. When I made something up on my own, like Gil laying out Hollis's cribbage hand, it felt good because you said "that's now true." I was never put on the spot too often. The ones I'd have frozen on were the open "tell us what's there" prompts in a row. Picking from the list first and then adding one detail worked best.

## Follow-up

**Q:** You said you never felt the numbers on your own dice. What would have made them feel real?

Mostly stakes stated before the roll. Most of the time I said what Harriet tried and then the result arrived as a finished piece of story: "a clean hit, here's the clue." I never had the beat of "if this goes wrong, here's what it costs, and here's what it takes to go right." So I couldn't tell a safe roll from a risky one. Dot's Night Move at the window was the one time I felt the tension, because you told Imogen exactly what she feared and offered her the choice to steady herself or look away.

Seeing the dice would help a bit, because I only ever got the totals after the fact and never saw 3 + 3 + 2 turn into something. But I think seeing them matters less than knowing what I was rolling for. If I'd heard "a 10 or more gets you the clue clean, a 7 to 9 gets it with a complication, and a miss means Etienne shuts the door," I'd have leaned in.

I'd also like to know the odds roughly, once. I was never sure whether +2 was a big deal. I'd have valued a one-line "that's a good bonus" early on.

And the Crowns would have made more sense if a miss had come with a threat I wanted to avoid. Most of my rolls were hits, so I never felt the temptation.
