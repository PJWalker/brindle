# Player interview: p1, Nina (Dot Fennimore-Pike)

Game g4, Dad Overboard, cozy/town-life style. Questions 1 to 9 from the brief, asked out of character after the session. Answers are verbatim.

## Answers

**1. Best moment.** The kitchen scene with Marian. Thirty-seven years of pie feud, then "I missed your rhubarb. Not the ribbon." Close second was the Etienne scene where Dot just held his hand and asked what he owed. Dot is a guidance counselor, so "I've heard worse in my office" felt like her. David and the blotter was the other one, because you let me make up something with real weight and then used it.

**2. What I didn't understand.**
- Advantage/disadvantage cancelling and the Conditions only clicked when you explained it with Marian's compass. Before that, "Condition" felt like a label with no teeth.
- Crowns: I got the idea (trade the roll for a scene later), but I only took one because I wanted the Marguerite scene. I couldn't tell if that was a smart use or a loophole. I also couldn't tell whether a Crown was "bad" to take. Crown of the Queen felt like a gift.
- The Cozy Little Place objects were fun, but I never really used mine. I forgot about the rolling pin and the recipe card. I'd want a reminder.
- Theorize: I could follow the math you said aloud, but I didn't feel like I understood the *structure* until you read the consensus back to us.

**3. What I thought the game wanted.** At first, a murder mystery with some cozy decoration. I realized around the fish market that it wanted *scenes with people*. Every roll turned into someone's feelings (Etienne, Lou, Sara, David). Once Lou asked about Nadine, I understood the town was the point. I loved that.

**4. Theorize.** It felt earned in that we really had 13 Clues. But honestly it was a bit fast and Tom-driven. Tom built the theory and Lena and I mostly amended it. I felt like I was agreeing to a chain I hadn't fully built. The "rolled 14" ending was a little anticlimactic, since the dice were never in doubt. The retcon for Sara's diary was my favorite part, because it made the Clue *mean* something. I understood "explained" fine as a rule, but I wasn't sure I'd know a Clue was explained if you hadn't told me.

**5. Void Clues.** They were the creepiest and my favorite part. The tattoo moving only when you aren't watching it, and the purple lamp, were great. The dream also got Marian's whole arc going. But I wasn't clear what to *do* with them, since they never count toward the theory. I want a payoff. The after-credits scene helped a lot. "The baker has the son" gave me chills.

**6. Pacing and spotlight.** I got plenty. If anything, I had a lot, because you leaned on Presence +2 and the pie/baker material. Cozy-versus-danger was a good mix. The town scenes (Etienne's chowder, Lou, Gil's bake sale) were my favorites. The case sometimes dragged me back from them, like when I wanted to stay on Lou and Nadine. I'd have liked one more pure town scene with no clue in it.

**7. Leads I couldn't follow.**
- Emily, the computer girl, who knew the safe numbers. We never talked to her.
- The two hooded young women, who I think are the same group. I'd have loved a scene with the bookstore girl.
- Hattie Blodgett and the porch. That got a Marian answer, but I wanted Hattie herself.
- Marguerite's answer. Obviously.

**8. One change.** Make the Cozy Move more prominent, maybe earlier and more than once. It was the best part, and it happened almost at the end because of time. I'd also add a gentle prompt about what each character wants from the other Mavens, since that's where the drama lives.

**9. Would I play again?** Yes, for sure. I want to see what the cult wants with "the baker's son." I'd also want a longer session so the relationships have room.

## Follow-up

**Q:** You said Theorize felt "Tom-driven" and you were agreeing to a chain you had not built. What would have let you build part of it?

Mostly it was you asking me first, plus a list in front of me. Here's what I mean.

**Ask each of us for one link before anyone commits.** When you said "you can try now," Tom had already been doing the math out loud for several turns, so by the time you turned to me, the chain existed and my job was to poke at it. If you'd said, "Nina, pick the Clue you care most about and tell us what it means," I'd have gone straight to Sara's diary or the paternity letter. Those are the people Dot actually met, so I had a real stake in them.

**A visible clue list.** I was holding eleven Clues in my head, and the only ones I could confidently use were the ones I'd found myself, plus whatever Tom summarized. A recap I could see, with a one-line note for each Clue (who found it, what it points at), would have let me spot connections on my own. The G.P. card and the will letter were obviously connected, but I only noticed after you read the list back.

**A different moment to start.** The Cozy Move was the right place for it, since we were already sitting at Dot's table. But it came *after* a lot of Tom's theory-building in the middle of scenes. I'd rather have waited until we were all in one room, with no one mid-action, and gone around the table.

I wouldn't change the consensus rule or the roll. I just wanted my turn at the chain to come before the commitment, not after.
