# Player interview: p2, Tom (Pru Pruitt)

Game g4, Dad Overboard, cozy/town-life style. Questions 1 to 9 from the brief, asked out of character after the session. Answers are verbatim.

## Answers

1. **Best moment:** Locked in Albert's study in the dark, then reading the safe dial with the binoculars turned backwards and getting the door open on a 7 with a complication. That was the one scene where the numbers I'd been carrying all night paid off. Second place is the confrontation line, "The sea doesn't pull a crown. A thumb does."

2. **What I didn't get:** I never rolled, so I had no feel for the dice, only what you told me afterwards. Advantage and disadvantage were fine once I saw them used on Marian, with the Condition and the compass cancelling. I was shaky on how Conditions get cleared, and on Crowns. Why would anyone put one on, and what exactly is the scene owed? I wasn't sure whether my "Furious" and "Rattled" would ever matter, since they only came up once. The Cozy Move I understood, but it felt like it belonged to Nina and Lena. I didn't do much in it.

3. **What the game wanted:** I thought it wanted a body and a puzzle, and for the most part it did. The surprise was that Clues come from conversation and meddling, not from hunting. I clocked that around the fish market, when I got the watch Clue without really searching.

4. **Theorize:** It felt earned and a bit too easy. Thirteen Clues against a threshold of three made the roll almost safe, and that took the tension out. I liked that we had to fit every Clue into the story, and that part was satisfying. I was never sure whether "explained" meant "fits" or "required." I'd like a plain rule: a Clue counts if the theory says why it exists. I also wondered how much you steered us, since you wrote up the consensus.

5. **Void Clues:** The tattoo that moves when you look away was the best creepy moment, because it was small and physical. The kneelers and the purple lamp were atmospheric, but I wanted them to matter to the murder, and I couldn't tell if they were supposed to. The cliffhanger ending made them make sense, but that was after the fact.

6. **Pacing:** Mostly good. Lou and Nadine, the chowder, and Gil's bake sale felt like a lot of side material. I know those are what the game is about, but when I only had a clock of an hour to work with, they slowed me down. Dot and Marian's kitchen scene was the exception. That was funny and moving. I got plenty of spotlight, though Pru ended up working the safe and the family while the other two had the town.

7. **Leads I couldn't follow:** Sorensen, who I wanted to chase from the first orange. The hooded girls, who handed me the numbers and then vanished. Emily, who we never actually interviewed even though she gave Sara the laptop numbers. And the other two husbands' deaths six years ago, which you flagged and then dropped. I wanted to pull the death certificates.

8. **One change:** Nudge us toward suspects sooner, and let each of the family members be a proper interview scene. We had five people on that yacht and spoke seriously to maybe two.

9. **Play again?** Yes. I'd bring the same character. I want Sorensen, I want the death certificates, and I want to roll the dice myself next time.

## Follow-up

**Q:** Was it the side scenes themselves, or that they were not yours? If each had a lead attached for Pru, would you have minded? Which bothered you most?

Mostly it was that they weren't mine and didn't point anywhere. Some of it is just me. I'm there for the puzzle, and a scene that doesn't move it forward feels like waiting for my turn. If each one had come with a lead attached for Pru, I'd have minded a lot less. Etienne's chowder is the proof. It was a festival detour on paper, but he was holding the skiff and the debt, so it never felt like a detour. I wanted to follow that thread.

The one that bothered me most was Lou and Nadine. It was a flowers-and-dancing advice scene while the clock was running and a body was waiting. The "water in lungs" tidbit buried in the middle was useful, but it was tucked inside the romance rather than being the point of the scene, and I almost missed it. If Lou had said "Doc Finch says no water in the lungs" first and asked about Nadine second, I'd have been fine.

I'd keep the town scenes if they carry a clue, or if they're short. The kitchen scene between Dot and Marian was long and had no case content, and I loved it because it was good character work, and because it was their moment, not a detour from mine. It's less that I dislike side scenes and more that I resent ones that are a pause with nothing in it.
