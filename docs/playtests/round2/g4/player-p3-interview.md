# Player interview: p3, Lena (Marian Teague)

Game g4, Dad Overboard, cozy/town-life style. Questions 1 to 9 from the brief, asked out of character after the session. Answers are verbatim.

## Answers

1. **Best moment.** Reading Walter's line in the logbook: "Didn't tell M." Then Gil telling the story of 1998 with Walter's father's compass on the windowsill. The lighthouse stopped being backstory and became the case. Close second was the kitchen scene where Marian admitted she'd let Dot stew over the pie. That felt like something these two women would really say.

2. **What I didn't understand.** Conditions and disadvantage, mostly. I got that a Condition hurts only when it would "plausibly" get in the way, but I couldn't have said in advance when that was. Using the compass to cancel it was clever, but I only understood it because you spelled it out. Crowns were fuzzy too. I didn't see why you'd ever take one, or how it differs from just failing. I never rolled, and I'm not sure why. I said what I tried and you told me the result. That was fine, but I'd have liked to know more about the numbers.

3. **What I thought it wanted.** Curiosity, and being kind to each other's characters. I realised around the kitchen scene that the point was the three women and the town, and that the murder was almost the excuse.

4. **Theorize.** It felt earned, but I didn't really understand "explained." It seemed to mean whatever we could stretch a story across, and the retcon for Sara's diary felt generous. I liked that, but I couldn't have told you where the line was. I'd have wanted that written down.

5. **Void Clues.** I loved them. The purple lamp scared me properly, and the dream of the crossword stayed with me all night. But "What the tide is owed" was never answered. I kept waiting for it. If it was meant to stay open, fine, but I noticed.

6. **Pacing and spotlight.** The cozy and the danger balanced well. The Deputy Lou and Nadine bits were my favourite town texture. But I felt idle a few times, especially while Dot and Pru were on the yacht and Marian waited by the phone. I'm quiet, and I didn't always know I was allowed to push.

7. **Leads I couldn't follow.** The kneelers, who they are and why Ray Gilchrist's wife is one. Walter and 1998. Hollis pegging short at cribbage. Duchess.

8. **One change.** Check in directly with quieter players between scenes, and give a one-page list of what counts as a Clue explained.

9. **Would I play again?** Yes, definitely. I want to find out what Walter did.

## Follow-up

**Q:** What would have told you that you were allowed to push: cutting to you sooner, a suggestion, a rule said at the start?

Mostly you cutting to me sooner with a direct question. Whenever you said "Lena, how does Marian take Walter's line?" or "what does she tell Wyman?", I knew exactly what to do and I was happy to do it. A question with her name on it is an invitation. When the scene was Dot and Pru and the yacht, I was waiting for the door to open, and nobody opened it.

A rule at the start would help too, but only if it were said out loud and plainly. Something like: "You can always ask me for a scene, or tell me where Marian is and what she's doing, even if it's not your turn." I read the "go anywhere, talk to anyone" line as being about moves. I didn't take it to mean I could jump in during someone else's scene. Part of that is me. I'm raised not to interrupt, and "idle" feels more polite than "pushy."

A suggestion of something to do would be the least useful of the three. I did have ideas, like the phone call to Wyman, but I only did them because the table was already moving. A prompt like "Marian, you're alone, what's on her mind?" would have been enough. I don't want a list of options. I'd just like to be asked.
