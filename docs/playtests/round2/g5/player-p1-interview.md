# Player interview: Chris (p1), played Dot Pruitt

g5, Dad Overboard, convention one-shot. Interviewed one-to-one after the session, out of character. Answers verbatim.

## Questions

1. What was your best moment?
2. Was there anything you didn't understand: the moves, the dice, advantage, Crowns, Conditions, the Cozy Little Place, Theorize?
3. What did you think the game wanted from you, and when did you realise it? Did my opening "how to play" pitch (go anywhere; Meddling finds Clues; Day/Night; Theorize at half the Complexity, and a hit makes it true; Void Clues; Crowns) help, and was anything in it wrong or missing?
4. Theorize: did it feel fair? Earned? Did you understand what counted as a Clue being "explained"?
5. The Void Clues (the moving tattoo, the chanting on the voicemail, the kneelers, the purple light): what did you make of them?
6. Pacing and spotlight: did you get enough? Did anyone dominate? How was the balance between cozy and danger? And the ending was cut short: how did that feel?
7. Were there leads you wanted to follow and couldn't?
8. If you could change one thing, what would it be?
9. Would you play again?

## Answers

1. **Best moment:** the Medical Bag roll at the body. A 13 meant I got the real cause of death, the stopped Rolex, and a moving tattoo all in the first scene, and I felt like I'd earned the room. A close second was the theory fight, when Benny's "more than one person" and Wendy's "Allison swung" collided and we merged them into the grip-and-swing version. That's when it felt like the three of us were solving it together.

2. **Confusion:** mostly small things. I never worked out what makes something a Clue. My ALIBI crossword deduction got bounced, and I only learned later that a Clue has to be a thing in the world. Crowns confused me at first, because "costs you a scene" was corrected to "mark a box." I also never used my Cozy Little Place objects (the banker's lamp, the medicine cabinet) for advantage, and I banked a Cozy Clue and never cashed it. That's on me, but it's also a lot of small systems.

3. **What the game wanted:** I came in wanting to win the mystery first. I realised around the index-card scene that it wants the table to agree on a story, and that a hit makes it *true*. Your opening pitch helped with the core loop. What was missing: that Clues must be concrete, that Theorize can't take advantage, and that Clues can be "explained away." The last one only came up at the end.

4. **Theorize:** fair and earned, and the roll was a hit with 12 Clues, so we were never in danger. I understood "explained" only after Wendy broke it down for me. Explaining away Walter's slip as Void texture felt a bit like a cheat, but I'd do it again.

5. **Void Clues:** I loved the tattoo shifting and the breathing chant. They're creepy and they got me wondering. I wanted to chase them, and being told they can't solve the case was mildly annoying. The Midwives scene at the end was great.

6. **Pacing:** I got a lot of spotlight, probably more than Benny, whose dice were cold. Wendy's odds-calling slowed me a little, but I came round to it. The scene cuts were good. Cozy to danger felt balanced. The cut-short ending was a shame, because I'd have liked to see the kneelers at the Point and David's scene played out.

7. **Leads:** Sorensen and who his Boston people are, Walter's 3-27-9-14, the extension click on Emily's call, Sara's ring, and the Pell and Pennywhistle folder.

8. **One change:** a one-page cheat sheet at the start. What counts as a Clue, what a Crown costs, and which moves can't touch Theorize.

9. **Again?** Yes, definitely.

## Follow-up

**Q:** You said you never used your Cozy Little Place objects and banked a Cozy Clue you never cashed. What would have made you remember? And did Theorize ever feel risky?

**A:**

What would have helped most is something I can see. I'd have those objects and the Cozy Clue written on an index card in front of me, or on the sheet, and ticked off when used. A prompt from you before rolls would help too, but I'm competitive and in a hurry, and I steamroll verbal reminders. Something physical on the table, like the Clue cards you kept, I'd actually notice. Small correction to my earlier answer: the lamp is Marjorie's. Dot's were the stopped clock (which I kept) and my own medicine cabinet. I used none of it, because in the moment it was always "roll, next scene." The Cozy Clue got lost when my ALIBI idea was bounced. I read that as "not now" and never went back.

On Theorize: no, it never felt risky. At 12 Clues it was a hit on any roll and about 92% for a clean one, and if it had missed we all had the Crown rescue. The tension was in the table agreeing on the *who*, which I enjoyed, but the dice were safe. The 9 gave us the mild complication, and the confrontation rolls, like Dot's 4, were the ones with real risk. If anything I'd have liked a harder call earlier, like having to decide whether to theorize on 6 Clues, because that's where the competitive part of me would have had a real choice.
