# Player interview: Wendy (p2), played Marjorie Pell

g5, Dad Overboard, convention one-shot. Interviewed one-to-one after the session, out of character. Answers verbatim.

## Questions

1. What was your best moment?
2. Was there anything you didn't understand: the moves, the dice, advantage, Crowns, Conditions, the Cozy Little Place, Theorize?
3. What did you think the game wanted from you, and when did you realise it? Did my opening "how to play" pitch (go anywhere; Meddling finds Clues; Day/Night; Theorize at half the Complexity, and a hit makes it true; Void Clues; Crowns) help, and was anything in it wrong or missing?
4. Theorize: did it feel fair? Earned? Did you understand what counted as a Clue being "explained"?
5. The Void Clues (the moving tattoo, the chanting on the voicemail, the kneelers, the purple light): what did you make of them?
6. Pacing and spotlight: did you get enough? Did anyone dominate? How was the balance between cozy and danger? And the ending was cut short: how did that feel?
7. Were there leads you wanted to follow and couldn't?
8. If you could change one thing, what would it be?
9. Would you play again?

## Answers

1. **Best moment:** Marjorie phoning Prentiss & Pike and getting the 12+. Then the voicemail drone, which she insisted was the engine. The other was the Cozy Move in Dot's kitchen, when I could name a Clue myself and the razored bell log was simply true. After a day of "the dice say," being told "no risk, it's true" felt good.

2. **Confusing bits:** Mostly boundaries, not mechanics.
   - Which ability fits an action. I asked about Reason vs. Composure vs. Presence several times and was glad you ruled it each time.
   - Columbo's "among the rich." I spent a turn working out whether the fish market counted.
   - What counts as a Clue. I understood after you told Chris that a deduction isn't one: it has to be a thing in the world.
   - Crowns. Your first pitch said "costs you a scene," which turned out loosely worded. The boxes and the Queen/Void difference I only got once you explained.
   - Gold Crown. I wanted to save it for Theorize, and the rule that it can't touch Theorize caught me out.

3. **What the game wanted:** To play a team of nosy women who collect things, then argue as a group about what the things mean. I realised it at the index cards, when the table's theory and the Keeper's Clues lined up. Your pitch helped, especially "a hit makes the theory true." Missing from it: what a Clue is, that you can Theorize again after a miss, "explained away," what Gold Crown can and can't do, and what Crown boxes cost. A pitch isn't a rulebook, but a one-page sheet would help.

4. **Theorize:** Fair and earned, yes, though not very tense. After I got to 11 Clues the roll was 2d6+5, a certain hit, so the only suspense was the 10+, and Benny's 9 was an 8% miss. If Complexity 6 is the standard, I'd like the table to feel the pressure of trying at 3 to 5 Clues. I did understand "explained": you said how each Clue got there or dropped it. I liked that Walter's slip stayed out.

5. **Void Clues:** The tattoo and voicemail unsettled me in the good way, and it was clever that they didn't count toward the solution. The kneelers at the Point, the purple lamp and the "Ask your husband's friends" line made me want Marjorie to confront Gerald's secret. I'm a skeptic by character, so I liked how little we were told.

6. **Pacing and spotlight:** I got plenty, perhaps a bit more than Dot, who missed twice. Nobody dominated. The cozy/danger balance was right: shortbread in the loft, a drowning risk on the forecastle. The cut-short ending was fine. The confrontation stayed intact, and I'd have regretted losing it.

7. **Leads not followed:** Walter's slip (3-27-9-14), Gerald and Walter in the 1998 photo, Nadine at the *Gazette*, what Sorensen's people really wanted, and what Albert promised in '98.

8. **Change one thing:** A one-page cheat sheet with the stats, the Theorize formula and the Crown/Gold Crown rules at the start.

9. **Play again:** Yes, definitely. Marjorie has a new cardigan to knit and a husband's secret to ask about.

## Follow-up

**Q:** You said Theorize wasn't tense. Would you have wanted to Theorize earlier at 5–6 Clues with real risk? Which rule should the opening pitch fix first?

**A:**

If we'd Theorized at 5 or 6 Clues, I would have wanted the risk. I'd have wanted it with the odds stated, the way you gave them. Waiting was itself the tension: at 5 Clues I worked out 42%, and I chose to go on gathering instead of rolling. Having that choice was the most fun part. The roll itself then felt like a formality, because by 11 Clues I'd made it one. So I like certainty, but I like earning it more. A Theorize at 6 Clues, 58% to hit and 17% to win clean, with the all-Crown rescue as the net, would have made the table hold its breath. A tidy ledger is satisfying, but I wouldn't have missed the chance to be wrong.

If I could fix one rule in the opening pitch, it would be **what counts as a Clue.** "A Clue is a thing in the world: something seen, found or heard, not a conclusion" would have saved Chris a turn and saved me from guessing. It shapes everything the players do and say all session. The runner-up is what Gold Crown can't do, since I nearly built a plan around it.
