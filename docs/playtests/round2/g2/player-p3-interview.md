# Player interview: Dev (p3, Winifred Aldous)

Game g2, house rules on. Interview taken one-on-one with say.sh after the session. The player's own words, lightly trimmed (formatting only).

## Main interview

Questions: (1) best moment; (2) anything not understood; (3) what the game wanted and when you realised; (4) Theorize fairness and the house rules; (5) Void Clues; (6) pacing, spotlight, cozy vs danger; (7) leads you couldn't follow; (8) one change; (9) play again?

Stepping out of character.

**1. Best moment.** Turning the slip handover into paperwork at the fish market: chain of custody, initials, a witness signature from Finch. It was an idea from my character's job, you ruled it needed no roll, and it worked. Second: the takedown, when Etienne wiped his hands on his apron twice and Winifred said "There it is." I'd planted that tell and it paid off.

**2. What I didn't understand.**
- **Meddling vs free talking** was blurry until you ruled in the moment. "Plain chat is free, prying is Meddling" is a good line, but I couldn't predict which side a question would fall on, and I only asked "who ran the launch" freely because Hollis happened to be friendly.
- **Crowns:** I understood the mechanic only at the end, when you explained the scene cost. Nobody used one, so I never felt the choice.
- **The Cozy Little Place** items never mattered to me. I had binoculars and a telescope and never used either for advantage, and I forgot they were mechanical at all. Jo and Marcus's objects came up sooner.
- **Conditions** were fine once they appeared.

**3. What the game wanted.** I thought early on it wanted me to collect Clues and assemble a theory, which is what I'd been wary of. The real shift came around 8 or 9 Clues, when I realized the work was making *one coherent story out of Clues the whole table found*, not finding the right answer. That's collaborative editing, not deduction. It took until the Sorensen/Hollis log to feel it.

**4. Theorize.** The strict counting was the whole check. At +5, the roll couldn't miss, so what could actually fail was the argument. That made it feel earned, and your throwing out Clue 12 was the moment I trusted it. The Facts vs Clues split helped on stakes, but it made free conversation feel like timeline only, and the clue budget made my 11 on the skiff feel like a consolation prize. Complexity 8 was fine in principle, but I'd want the final roll to stay risky even at 12 or 13 explained. I did mind that the majority ruled on the wire, Etienne's call and the Polaroid. My version was just as consistent, and it felt a little deflating that two votes could overrule me when all three hung together.

**5. Void Clues.** Great images, the moving tattoo and the purple-eyed fish, and nearly no mechanical weight tonight. The purple lighthouse beam and the midwives scene did suggest a plan, so I'll give it credit, but nothing I found with a Void Clue ever changed a roll, a scene or a choice. I wanted one to bite.

**6. Pacing and spotlight.** Good. I got plenty, and Odalie's Emily thread was the emotional centre. The cozy/danger balance leaned cozy. The deadline clocks felt abstract; nobody was ever in physical danger, so the "real danger" XP question stayed a no.

**7. Leads I couldn't follow.** Sorensen mentioning Leonard in front of me, and my "later" column. I never got back to it. Gil Wexford ("G.W.") from the 1998 log, which got cut from the theory. Duchess's return, the engine room chanting, Emily's online "money writer," and the Polaroid's real source.

**8. One change.** Make the Theorize roll risky even at a high clue count. Either cap the bonus or make the Complexity scale with the number of suspects, so that 13 Clues feels like 2d6, not a foregone conclusion.

**9. Play again?** Yes. The strict counting made it meaningful, and I want to see whether the Void actually pays off.

## Follow-up

Q: How should the Keeper have reached consensus instead of majority? And on the clue budget: known from the first scene, or not at all?

**On consensus:** I'd have the Keeper settle it on explanatory merit, not a head count. Take the wire. Ask what each version needs us to invent, and which one makes Sorensen's line ("somebody on that boat moved Albert's money") and the 12:07 timing work with fewer extras. If Albert sent it himself, Sorensen's remark is odd, because he'd have known. If Andrew did it, the remark is a creditor fishing. A test like that would have felt like a ruling, not a vote. I'd also give the player who owns a Clue's origin the first say. Winifred got the Polaroid via Scarecrow, so my read on who handed it over should have counted for more than two guesses at who "might" have sent it. Where two versions hang equally well, flip a coin or let the Keeper pick and say why. Majority rule is weak when two players like a version out of loyalty to each other.

**On the budget:** I'd want to know from the first scene. Not knowing made my 11 on the skiff feel like a consolation prize and changed how I valued the earlier hits, because I'd been counting toward a ceiling I couldn't see. My preference is no hard cap at all, with the risk handled at the Theorize roll instead. If you do want a cap, tell us up front and show the running count in each recap, so we can decide whether to push for another Clue or Theorize now.
