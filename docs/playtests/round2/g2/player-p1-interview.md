# Player interview: Marcus (p1, Hortense Pilkington-Vale)

Game g2, house rules on. Interview taken one-on-one with say.sh after the session. The player's own words, lightly trimmed (formatting only).

## Main interview

Questions: (1) best moment; (2) anything not understood; (3) what the game wanted and when you realised; (4) Theorize fairness and the house rules; (5) Void Clues; (6) pacing, spotlight, cozy vs danger; (7) leads you couldn't follow; (8) one change; (9) play again?

Out of character, answers in order:

1. **Best moment:** Pinning Andrew on the watch. A small fact, that a butler winds the household clocks every Sunday, ended up carrying the reveal, and it felt like the kind of detail an escape room hides in plain sight. Close second was the argument with Dev over the wire and the skiff, because we were negotiating a fair answer. Third, the mantel piece in the Cozy Move.

2. **What I didn't understand:**
   - **Meddling vs. free chat.** The line moved. Bev was Presence (charm) and I'd argued Reason. I only got the Facts vs. Clues split midway through, and it should have been up front.
   - **Cozy Little Place objects.** I never used any. I forgot they existed and nobody reminded us.
   - **Conditions.** They cleared when "the fiction says so," which felt loose.
   - **Crowns.** "A scene later" is vague, so I never wanted to buy one.
   - **Mulder.** I never learned when it was worth spending, or whether the converted Clue needed a job in the theory.

3. **What the game wanted:** Collect Clues and build a theory. I realised about halfway, around the 11:52 call and the wire. What took longer is that the answer isn't pre-written. We built it. That's the opposite of what I came for. I wanted a real answer to find, and the strict counting is the only reason it still felt earned.

4. **Theorize:** Earned, yes. Fair, partly. Facts vs. Clues and "say why it exists, who left it, what it proves" were the best rules of the night. But at 13 explained Clues the modifier was +5 and the roll couldn't miss, so the strictness was the whole test. Dropping 12 while accepting 14 felt a bit arbitrary, since both hung on Dev's cult hook. And settling disputes by majority vote worked, but it wasn't a deduction. The Clue budget I only noticed late, and I'd have wanted it stated at the start. Complexity 8 helped, but not enough.

5. **Void Clues:** The creepiest bits (the moving tattoo, the fish) but mechanically inert. As a deduction player, they felt like pieces from a different puzzle. The ending suggests they matter later, but within the session I got no payoff.

6. **Pacing and spotlight:** Plenty for me, and the split scenes worked. But the balance tilted heavily toward investigation. After I handed the Sheriff evidence, both clocks paused and nothing felt dangerous. I wanted more creep.

7. **Leads I couldn't follow:** Sara (the nail file) and David, whom we never spoke to. Emily's "someone who writes about money." The Polaroid's rain-hat man. Sorensen's remark about Leonard, which I flagged and nobody picked up. Gil Wexford at the lighthouse. Tiago's sound below deck.

8. **One change:** Make the Theorize roll able to miss, either by capping how many Clues count or by raising Complexity. A theory that can't fail turns the final roll into a formality.

9. **Play again:** Yes, mainly for the strict-counting theory-building. I'd like next time to see whether the Void side gets teeth.

## Follow-up

Q: You said dropping Clue 12 while accepting 14 felt arbitrary. What would have made it feel fair? And would you rather the house rules had been on a printed sheet from the start?

Both, but the stated test matters more than the chance to argue.

**The test.** I'd want one sentence, applied to every Clue: "A Clue counts if your theory says what it proves about something that happened on Sunday night, in terms of who, when or how." By that test, 14 (lights at the Point at 1:08) and 12 (Alistair's 1998 log) look closer than the rulings suggested. Both are about Albert and the Point, but 12 is about 1998 and 14 is about Sunday, so a test like that would have separated them cleanly and I'd have accepted the result.

**The chance to argue.** I did get some. Dev's hook rescued 14, but 12 got a ruling without the same invitation. I could have argued that 12 explains why the body was sent to the Point, since Albert had been pulled out there before and the town "left it alone." I'd rather have been asked to make that case, even if I'd lost it.

**A printed sheet.** Yes, one page. Meddling vs. free chat, Facts vs. Clues, what "explained" means, the Clue budget, and majority rule for contested details. The rest I'm happy to learn by playing, but those four rules were learned by trial and error at the table, and I'd rather have spent that time on the mystery.
