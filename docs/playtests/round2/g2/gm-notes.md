## Verdict on the house rules (r-house)
**Net positive for this table, but they don't do the job the app says they're for.**
- **Strict counting**: the clear winner. It turned Theorize into the session's best scene and answered the skeptic's "nothing matters" worry (p3: ruling out Clue 12 "was the first moment I believed it could be honest"). It needs a stated one-sentence test and a consensus/tiebreak method, or it feels arbitrary.
- **Talking is free**: made NPC scenes faster and more in-character (p2 loved it), and made pushing an explicit, declared choice. But it (a) clashes with the book's "be very generous", (b) creates the undefined Facts layer, and (c) cut the number of rolls and misses, so danger and Conditions dropped. Best combined with the book's "Are you trying to trigger the Meddling Move?".
- **NPCs deflect / bold questions earn something**: worked every time. It's a small rule and an easy keeper. The NPC `guarded` fields made it cheap to run.
- **Spread suspicion**: useful as a clue-picking heuristic, and invisible to players. Players still converged on the household, so it delays convergence rather than preventing it.
- **Clue budget**: it bit once (Winifred's 11 became a sharpening), and that felt like "a consolation prize" because it wasn't announced from scene one. It also doesn't cover the Cozy Move, which put three Clues straight back.
- **Complexity 8**: necessary but not sufficient. It raised the Theorize threshold to 4 and made players hunt for more Clues, which was good. But 13 counted still gave +5, a roll that can't miss. Strict counting, not Complexity, was the real check.
- **Clashes with the book seen**: talking-free vs "be very generous" (ex6, ex9, ex13); the clue budget vs the book's "every hit is a Clue"; Complexity 8 vs the published 6. None broke play, but each needed a ruling the app didn't give.
- **For new players**: better than the book on Theorize (felt earned) and on social scenes; worse on danger and on knowing what a roll will get you. Run strict counting and NPC deflection always. Run talk-is-free with the book's question. Drop or rework the budget.

## Top 5 recommended changes (ranked)
1. **Give strict counting a one-sentence test and a consensus method in mv-theorize.gm / r-house.** Suggested test: "It counts if the theory says why it exists, who left it, and what it proves about who, when or how on the night." Method: settle splits on which version needs fewer inventions; the player whose Clue it is speaks first; the Keeper breaks ties and says why. No head-counts.
2. **Make Theorize able to miss, or say plainly that it can't.** Options for r-house: Clues that count beyond the Complexity add nothing (cap the modifier at +2), or count only one Clue per Suspect-thread. Or tell Keepers that at +5 the real check is the counting, and spend the 7–9 vs 10+ on the confrontation.
3. **Define Facts vs Clues for "talking is free" in r-house**: free and favour information goes on the timeline and helps a Clue fit, but doesn't add +1. Pair the rule with the book's "Are you trying to trigger the Meddling Move?", and add a `guarded` line to npc-bev (and an Ida entry).
4. **Fix the clue budget**: announce it to players at the start with a running count in every recap, and state whether it applies to Cozy Move and Scarecrow Clues. Better: replace it with option 2 and drop it.
5. **Fix the data errors**: Gerald Pratt → Prentiss (npc-albert); merge npc-lavoie into npc-hale; align Sorensen's times; add a Layer One threshold note to r-flow/mv-void; put Maven moves (with one-per-table rules) in the moves reference; add a roll-time reminder for Cozy Little Place items and a concrete Crown example in r-pitch.
