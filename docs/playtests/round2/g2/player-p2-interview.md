# Player interview: Jo (p2, Dame Odalie Fenwick-Pruitt)

Game g2, house rules on. Interview taken one-on-one with say.sh after the session. The player's own words, lightly trimmed (formatting only).

## Main interview

Questions: (1) best moment; (2) anything not understood; (3) what the game wanted and when you realised; (4) Theorize fairness and the house rules; (5) Void Clues; (6) pacing, spotlight, cozy vs danger; (7) leads you couldn't follow; (8) one change; (9) play again?

**1. Best moment.** The bookshop scene with Emily. I dropped the big voice and just sat with a scared kid over tea, and I got a 12 on the roll, so the scene rewarded me for being gentle. Close second: Sorensen knowing the cat's name. The final "Places at eleven" speech was fun, but the quiet scene was the one I'll remember.

**2. What I didn't understand.**
- **Cozy Little Place:** I never used the prop trunk or the Macbeth dagger. I forgot they existed until the recap, and I think I didn't know *when* to reach for them. A nudge like "is there an object here that could help?" would have helped.
- **Crowns:** I understand them on paper, but I never felt the pull. Keeping the bad rolls was always more fun, so I didn't test the system.
- **Conditions:** I got the idea, but "disadvantage when you need to concentrate" was vague. I wasn't sure when it applied, so I flagged it myself and hoped for a ruling.
- **Remington Steele:** I wasn't sure how much the move gave me beyond "the disguise is free."
- **Theorize:** I followed it, but Marcus and Dev did most of the arithmetic.

**3. What the game wanted.** I think it wanted us to chase leads, trade information, and then argue a story into being as a group. I got that around the second or third Clue, when the Sheriff said "give me one thing." Honestly, I didn't feel the pull to *solve* it. I was happier building the town and the scenes, and I felt the game let me do that, which I didn't expect.

**4. Theorize.** It felt fair and earned. Making us say *why* each Clue exists and who left it kept it from being a lottery. I liked that talking was free and that Facts and Clues were separate, because I could chat with Ida without worrying about rolling. The downside: at +5 it couldn't really miss, so the tension was in the discussion, not the dice. I'd say Complexity 8 with strict counting is about right, but a miss should be possible. The clue budget made sense once explained, but I didn't see it coming.

**5. Void Clues.** I loved them as flavour: the moving tattoo and the purple-eyed fish are the images I'm still thinking about. Mechanically they did nothing, which frustrated Dev and mildly frustrated me. I wanted them to *do* something, even a small effect.

**6. Pacing and spotlight.** I got plenty. Cozy vs danger leaned heavily cozy: nothing really threatened us until the Sorensen scene, and even that was only embarrassing. I'd turn up the risk a notch, though the cosy side was great. Hortense and Winifred drove the evidence, and I drove the people. It balanced out.

**7. Leads I couldn't follow.** The "prayer people" at the Point and the humming below deck. Sorensen's employers. Emily's mysterious online "money" friend. And I'd have loved a longer Lillian Voss con.

**8. One change.** Give the Void Clues a small mechanical hook, something tangible like a die bonus or a consequence. They're the best part of the setting and the least rewarded.

**9. Play again?** Yes. I came for the voices and the scenes, and I got both, plus a pillow-stitching cat-wrangler I'm fond of. I'd like to find out what's under the Point.

## Follow-up

Q: Concretely, what should Void Clues do (a dice effect, a Condition, a scene, a choice)? And did 'talking is free' change how you played the NPC scenes?

**Void Clues.** I'd want a scene or a choice, not a dice effect. Dice bonuses are what I'd forget about anyway, like the prop trunk. What I'd enjoy is the weirdness reaching back out at me. For example, when Odalie holds the moving-tattoo Clue, the Keeper could offer a little uncanny moment: the spiral shows up on something in her own home, or Sir Laurence stares at it, or she hears something in the wings that she shouldn't. Then I choose how Odalie reacts. That gives me something to perform. A Condition would also work if it were as specific as SPIRAL BEHIND MY EYES, but that one only felt good because I invoked it myself. I'd be happy if a Void Clue sometimes handed me a decision, like "this will help the case, but it costs you a bit of your nerve." If I had to pick one, scene first, choice second, dice last.

**Talking is free.** It changed how I played a lot, and for the better. With Ida, Bev, Philippa and Hollis, I could just ask the next natural question and stay in the voice. If every chat had needed a roll, I think I'd have stopped to check my numbers and asked fewer, bigger questions, and I'd have rushed the shopkeeper scenes. It also made it obvious when I was *pushing*, because the Keeper said so and the tone shifted. The one risk is that I leaned on being charming and got most of what I wanted without paying for it. That was fun for me, but I can imagine a table that finds it too easy.
