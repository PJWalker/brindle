# Dad Overboard — round 3 playtests (slice of life)

There were four simulated 3-hour sessions. Each was one Sonnet instance playing the Keeper and three players, working from `js/seed.js` only.

The Keepers were told to:
- pitch the game as slice of life, with players taking the initiative and following their impulses instead of going in rounds;
- run the session in scenes;
- split the Mavens up;
- run town and personal challenges alongside the case.

Raw files are in `raw/round3/gN/`: transcript, Keeper notes, dice log and Keeper interview.

| Game | Keeper | Players | Clues (mundane + Void) | Theorize |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| g1 | warm, improv | shy first-timer, improv ham, cozy-mystery note-taker | 7 + 2 | declined (about 5 clues counted against Complexity 8) |
| g2 | D&D veteran, new to this game | waits for his turn, optimiser, chaos player | 8 + 3 | not ready |
| g3 | quiet, personal scenes | married couple who stick together, secretive loner | 3 + 1 | out of reach |
| g4 | dramatic, leans into the Void | cozy/romance player, Void chaser, competitive solver | 6 + 4 | deferred to "Friday" |

**No table reached a Theorize roll.** Session zero (pitch, safety, character creation) took 30–45 minutes everywhere.

## The instructions, at the table
- **Slice of life worked best when a personal stake collided with the case.**
  - g2: a crossword rival.
  - g4: the harbourmaster, Hollis, courting one of the Mavens.
  - g3: an old flame, a dahlia feud, and a grandson who turned up.
  - g1: an acting rival.

  Clues arrived sideways through these. Scenes with no stake attached fell flat, and so did pure recaps.
- **Opening each scene with what the NPC was doing in their own life** (g4) gave the best town play. Nobody asked an NPC for a clue outright.
- **No rounds:** this was easy at most tables. The D&D player needed nudging until about 1:30.
- **Splitting** works, but "split up" combined with "no rounds" turned into one Maven per scene with the other two as audience (g1). The couple resisted being split (g3). The loner hid a clue, and the seed has no rule for a clue that's found but not shared.
- **Non-mystery challenges** made up 30–40% of scenes, and they were often the most memorable. Keeper moves on failed rolls were remembered more than the successes (g4).
- **Cost:** with slice-of-life pacing, tables found 4–10 clues in 3 hours, against the seed's target of 10–14 and Complexity 8.

## What surprised the Keepers
- g1: Dev's stool speech in the diner earned Hollis's logbook. Shy Maureen took off once she was given a personal hook. Nobody went aboard the yacht in 3 hours.
- g2: Sam swam to the yacht, stole a spoon, and accepted a Crown of the Void with "yeah, sure". The best scene grew out of Gary's crossword rival.
- g3: a player judged the chowder contest instead of rolling for the autopsy. A player invented a dead husband mid-scene. A player claimed a clue that doesn't exist.
- g4: a player invented a torn diary page. The players connected the spiral thread themselves. A Maven promised Tommy secrecy, then told her friends.

## Seed issues (number of tables)
1. **No character-creation rules** (4/4): no ability array, playbooks, Condition effects, advance list or Crown mechanics. Every Keeper improvised.
2. **Clue budget against Complexity at 3 hours** (4/4): a 10–14 clue target with Complexity 8 is unreachable at slice-of-life pace. Either lower the Complexity, or say that a first session usually ends without Theorize and explain how to close it.
3. **Contradictions** (4/4):
   - Philippa Hale and Prudence Lavoie are both Inn event managers.
   - The lawyer is Prentiss in one place and Pratt in another.
   - "Several nights" vs "three nights".
   - The Ball is "next week" but also on the wedding Saturday.
   - Andrew's service is 20 years in one place and 35 in another.
   - Sorensen's times vary: 12:20, 12:25, 12:40.
   - Tiago is both skipper and deckhand.
   - Etienne's skiff is in two places.
4. **No in-game calendar** (4/4): t5's "per day" tick, the Ball, the wedding, the vote and the Friday deadline have no fixed days.
5. **Threat triggers** (4/4):
   - t4 fills in one Void-heavy session.
   - t2's "tick at scene changes" is ambiguous.
   - t1's "something solid" is undefined.
6. **The Void thread hinges on c16** (3/4): c16 was found once, and it has to be both a compass bearing and a safe combination. v1, v5 and v6 have no notes.
7. **Missing "life right now"** (g1): Bev, the Krauses, Tiago, Prentiss and Hale have none, though the town-life rule cites Bev as its example.
8. **Rule conflict** (g4): the split rule ("cut every exchange or two") clashes with the town-life rule ("don't cut away from personal beats").
9. **Establishing Question** (g2, g4): it is singular, but three players each pick a different child. g2's Keeper put the wrong child in the scene.
10. **Weak clue placement** (g3):
    - c3 comes straight from the Sheriff's file early.
    - Finch's autopsy facts aren't on the clue list.

## Keeper lessons for future sessions
- Cap session zero at 15–20 minutes. Collect one personal stake per player there, and write each stake and Establishing answer on a card.
- Before play, fix the calendar and decide what a Crown costs.
- Frame each scene as a place, a pressure and two Mavens where possible. Give the watching players jobs: voice an NPC, set the weather. Cut on a cliffhanger or once a personal beat finishes, not on a count.
- At each cut, read the shared clue list aloud and say which Maven knows what. Agree up front how secret clues work.
- The player names the action and ability before the roll. Use 7–9 results for threat ticks and hard choices, and 10+ for leverage.
- Let an NPC say "Void" in the story first. A Void clue ticks t4 at most once per scene. Put c16 somewhere the players are sure to pass.

## Changes the Keepers suggested
- **Seed:** a "table kit" at the front: a character-creation page, Condition, Crown and advance rules, the calendar, and a recommended clue count and Complexity for 3 hours. Also fix the contradictions.
- **Instructions:**
  - Replace "actively split the Mavens up" with "give each scene a place, a pressure and two Mavens; keep a cut ready mid-scene".
  - Build "ask each player for a stake" into the pitch.
  - Say that ending without a Theorize roll is normal.

## Caveat
One model played the Keeper and every player at each table, so "surprises" are moments where the character voices went somewhere the Keeper hadn't planned. The shared scratchpad led three tables to log some rolls to g1's dice log by mistake. The rows were moved back to the right logs, and the dice values are real.
